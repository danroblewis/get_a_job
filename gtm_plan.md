## Section 3: Discovery Call Script

**Duration:** 20-30 minutes
**Goal:** Understand the pain, confirm fit, propose a next step. Not a close.

---

### Open (2 minutes)

> "Thanks for making time. I'll keep this tight — 20-25 minutes, and I'll leave room for your questions at the end.
>
> Here's how I'd like to run it: I want to spend most of the time understanding your situation, because the only way I can tell you whether I can actually help is if I understand what you're dealing with. Is that okay?"

---

### Their Situation (5 minutes)

> "So tell me — what does your ops stack look like right now? What tools is your team actually running in day to day?"

*Listen. Let them talk. Note: Notion, Airtable, Slack, Gmail, Salesforce, HubSpot, Intercom, Zendesk.*

> "And how big is the ops team — or is it you and a few others wearing ops hats?"

*Listen. Smaller = higher likelihood that pain is personal and urgent.*

> "What's the nature of the work? Are you mostly managing processes, managing data, managing people — or all three?"

---

### Pain Depth (10 minutes)

> "You mentioned [X tool or X process]. Walk me through what that actually looks like for someone on your team on a typical week. Like, step by step — what are they doing?"

*Listen for: manual data entry, copy-paste between tools, someone manually building reports, meeting notes that don't get actioned, onboarding that depends on one person's memory.*

> "How long does that take, roughly?"

> "And that's happening how often — daily? Weekly?"

*Do the math in your head. If it's 2 hours/week, that's 100 hours/year. Keep that number ready.*

> "What happens when it doesn't get done? Or when it gets done wrong?"

*This is where pain depth reveals itself. If the answer is "nothing really" — lower urgency. If the answer is "customers notice" or "deals fall through" — high urgency.*

> "Is this something you've tried to fix before? What happened?"

*Listen for: Zapier that broke, a contractor who didn't deliver, an intern who left.*

> "If this was solved — if that process just ran automatically and your team didn't have to touch it — what does that actually free up for you?"

*Let them articulate the value themselves. Don't sell here. Just listen.*

---

### Fit Check (5 minutes)

> "Okay, let me be honest with you about what I do and whether it fits.
>
> I build Claude Code automations that plug into the tools you already have. The deliverable is a working workflow — tested on your real data, with documentation, and a live handoff. I don't leave you with a recommendation doc.
>
> Based on what you've told me, what you're describing with [specific process they mentioned] sounds like something I could scope into a 2-3 week project. The way I'd approach it is [brief, specific description of approach].
>
> The thing I can't help with is [anything outside scope — e.g., if they need a full product build, if they need ongoing dedicated support, if it requires deep ERP integrations]. If that's the main thing, I want to tell you that now rather than waste your time."

> "Does that match what you were hoping to solve, or is there a gap between what you described and what you actually need?"

---

### Next Step (3 minutes)

> "Here's what I'd like to suggest. I'll put together a short scope document — one page — that describes what I'd build, how long it would take, and what it costs. No obligation on your end. It'll give you something concrete to react to rather than talking in abstractions.
>
> I can have that to you within 48 hours. Does that work?"

*If they hesitate:*

> "Alternatively, if you want to start smaller — I have an entry engagement at $500 where I scope and validate the problem before building anything. Some people prefer to start there before committing to a full project."

> "Either way — what's the best email to send it to?"

If what you're doing with Zapier is working, I wouldn't suggest replacing it.
